## What the existing verifier proves

The [qualified protocol contract](../split-core/team-vault-keys.md#authenticated-authority-and-current-state-selection) defines opaque account IDs, generation-bound X25519/Ed25519 identities, signed authority transitions and separate signed vault snapshot heads. Identity self-signatures establish key possession, not a wallet relationship. Current code checks strict Ed25519 over the complete canonical `T("signed-record", ...)` message, with explicit canonical/non-small-order checks on public key and signature R: `crates/bitbid-core/src/team_vault/crypto.rs:8–32`. Account identity generation has no chain or wallet field: `account.rs:147–180`.

The pure verifier checks previous authority, exact sequence/action/roster/policy/ref sets, current actor powers, required recipient acceptances, sealed vault heads and allowed snapshot/content relations: `protocol.rs:472–882`. `RecordSet::verify_pinned` checks local accepted ancestry; `verify_anchored` checks the trusted-peer bootstrap ceremony: `state.rs:111–169`. Neither supplies chain selection/finality. `Activation::reconcile` accepts a caller-supplied observed digest and knows no transaction receipt or block: `state.rs:374–387`. The [protocol's freshness limits](../split-core/team-vault-keys.md#typed-export-roots-closure-and-verification) expressly distinguish a valid history from independently established latest state.

These facts require a conjunction: a live client needs a valid qualified cryptographic history **and** an independently accepted canonical chain checkpoint selecting its terminal authority and vault heads. A chain hash alone cannot fabricate the first condition; the pure verifier alone cannot establish the second. Historical exports remain historical reading, not current authority.

## What bounded public projection enforcement can establish

**Design inference:** a new contract can enforce wallet-authorized role/permission changes, bounded state relations and atomic compare-and-swap while clients verify Ed25519 and recipient encryption. It must do substantially more than store an opaque hash beside a claimed projection:

1. Validate domain-bound wallet/account/key registration and the action's current wallet authority. Bind registry/version/chain and replay/cancellation inputs. Direct transactions still require exact caller/account/current-generation checks. If typed signatures are used, [EIP-712](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-712) supplies structured domain fields but leaves replay handling to the application; specify checked nonces/expected heads and deadlines rather than treating the standard as replay protection.
2. Parse or deterministically reconstruct the **exact canonical v1 public record bytes** from the bounded submitted fields, including kind, field/list order, widths, signature bytes and refs. Recompute the v1 SHA256 wire/plan hashes and prove that the projections used for decisions are those committed bytes. A caller-supplied ABI/JSON roster plus an unrelated authority hash is not such a proof. Use the existing v1 framing; ordinary ABI encoding is not interchangeable with its T framing. Solidity exposes SHA256, but its ABI/packed encodings have their own rules and packed dynamic data can be ambiguous. [Solidity cryptographic functions](https://docs.soliditylang.org/en/latest/units-and-global-variables.html#mathematical-and-cryptographic-functions), [ABI specification](https://docs.soliditylang.org/en/latest/abi-spec.html).
3. Enforce the public action/diff/cardinality rules and bind every authorization-relevant field to the canonical record. Required recipient wallet approval can add an independent chain-level consent check, but cannot be described as verification of the existing Ed25519 acceptance. Any extra consent/online requirement must be explicit and preserve the selected product workflows.
4. Ordinary content publication compares current authority and that vault's head. Authority transitions compare the old authority and all current affected/required heads, seal old head extensions, and activate the complete new state atomically. Staging never becomes authority. Exactly one competing transition wins. Bounds must be fixed and fail closed; no silent partial state or merely client-enforced ACL in place of contract rights.
5. Clients independently verify every required v1 identity/signature/public relation, bootstrap pins and selected-body decryption. They reject mismatched projections, invalid signatures, stale or withheld required records even when the wallet transaction succeeds.

The parser/reconstruction equivalence, scope/capacity and gas feasibility are **unproven**. Hashes authenticate bytes; they do not prove public semantics unless the contract validates their binding, and do not prove availability, correct recipient encryption or identical plaintext during rotation. The [existing protocol](../split-core/team-vault-keys.md#removal-rotation-and-lifecycle-failure-cases) already states that a public signature cannot compel working grants or establish secret erasure.

## Residual authorized invalid-head denial

**Design inference:** without checking Ed25519, a wallet currently allowed to mutate a scope can supply structurally correct bytes containing an invalid Ed25519 signature. The chain can accept its public projection/hash while every correct core client rejects that head. An ordinary vault writer may thereby block that vault's next valid head and operations that must seal its latest head. A privileged authority writer can block wider team progress. This is denial of availability, not permission for clients to accept forged cryptographic history.

Even full public Ed25519 verification does not eliminate all authorized denial: a permitted writer can sign unusable ciphertext, and a grantor can construct recipient ciphertext that does not yield the committed key. A contract cannot check private decryption correctness or plaintext preservation merely from a valid signature. Independent copies, backups and explicit failure handling remain necessary. The public-validation omission nevertheless adds a distinct invalid-signature denial surface and must be named rather than hidden inside a generic IPFS outage disclaimer.

## Recovery without silently discarding accepted pins

There is no demonstrated general same-team reset for a client-verification-only design:

- Keep the last core-accepted authority/head, canonical transaction/block evidence, rejected bytes and any grant-disclosure history. Do not install a rejected hash as a cryptographic pin or call a failed signature a temporary success.
- A contract cannot safely decide that an older head is the last globally valid head solely because a repair caller says so. A proposed rollback may discard a valid descendant another client already accepted. Existing `verify_pinned` rejects omission/fork of observed accepted heads; do not reset its pin to force acceptance.
- Before finalization, cancellation or failed submission can leave the prior chain head current, but any grant bytes already disclosed still require reconciliation/rotation treatment. This is not a repair of an already finalized invalid head.
- A general in-place repair needs an independently enforceable invalidity proof, a contract-defined pre-acceptance challenge protocol, or a newly accepted recovery authority/trust rule. None exists in the current source. A repair that skips an invalid predecessor changes chain selection semantics and requires explicit design, acceptance and testing.
- The safe baseline failure path is to freeze the affected live workflows and expose historical recovery. Where surviving authorized users have a readable valid backup, an **explicit new-team migration** can create a new team ID/genesis, fresh vault keys, verified recipients and fresh grants, then import recovered plaintext locally as new current records. Label the old team/head as incident history; do not present the new genesis as continuation of the old pin. Reconcile current canonical account/authorization prerequisites, and do not infer that an export or recovery code restores old roles. This is a proposed recovery contract requiring a bounded continuation, not a feature already available.
- Missing readable records/root/recovery material can make recovery impossible. A new registry entry cannot reconstruct ciphertext or secret keys. Last-Owner loss is separately constrained: qualified v1 starts with one Owner, provides transfer rather than adding another Owner, and has no operator/social reset action. [Qualified action table](../split-core/team-vault-keys.md#exact-finalized-authority-transition-relation).

## Alternatives

| Approach | Additional guarantee | Required assumptions / limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact bounded public projection + wallet ACL/CAS; client Ed25519/AEAD checks | Contract prevents unauthorized wallet/diff/CAS changes; clients reject invalid crypto | Authorized invalid-signature head denial remains. Accept explicit freeze/new-team recovery and possible irrecoverability; prove projection equivalence/gas/capacity. No automatic same-team reset. |
| Contract verifies every required public v1 signature and transition before head selection | Prevents invalid signatures and omitted public transition rules from becoming a selected head | Must qualify exact strict Ed25519/canonical parsing, all referenced newly introduced records, bounds and gas on the selected fork. Still cannot prove private encryption correctness or data availability. |
| Verifiable computation bridge for the same public verifier | Could move expensive verification off-chain while enforcing its result on-chain | A concrete audited proof system/program, proof-to-input/state binding, verifier contract, resource/cost and failure policy would be new work. No such bridge is implemented or validated here; do not invent one. |
| Optimistic proposal/challenge period | Can prevent challenged invalid candidates becoming finalized if the dispute mechanism is sound and observed in time | Requires public data availability, a complete enforceable invalidity predicate/proof, challenge availability, timing/bond/economic and censorship assumptions. Heads must remain pending; clients must not pin/use a candidate as final before the window closes. A naive rollback after clients accept recreates the pin conflict. |

### Ed25519 feasibility evidence

[EIP-665](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-665) is marked Stagnant. Its proposed interface signs a 32-byte message; it is not evidence that a cheap primitive is available on the selected chain or that it validates this protocol's arbitrary-length pure-Ed25519 messages. Prehashing the existing message to fit a different interface changes what is signed and is not an allowed transparent substitution.

The inspected [Bor development precompile table](https://github.com/0xPolygon/bor/blob/develop/core/vm/contracts.go) maps SHA256 and ECDSA recovery, and its shown Istanbul/Berlin/Cancun/Prague tables map address 0x09 to BLAKE2F, not the proposed Ed25519 interface. This is repository-source evidence, **not** identification of the actual Amoy/PoS deployed fork/version. Exact target capabilities and implementations must be pinned and exercised. Neither an EIP's suggested gas price nor a development source table proves deployment suitability.

### Optimistic comparison evidence

The [OP Stack fault-proof specification](https://specs.optimism.io/fault-proof/) describes an actual trace/dispute/step-verification mechanism. Its [Portal specification](https://specs.optimism.io/fault-proof/stage-one/optimism-portal.html) ties finalization to valid games and proof maturity checks. Those are examples of the machinery and assumptions needed, not reusable proof that Bitbid can challenge Ed25519/public-v1 invalidity cheaply. A challenge period without an enforceable terminal verifier or available data is not a verification design. Optimistic acceptance also introduces delayed publication semantics and must explicitly scope immediate local fail-closed removal while canonical finalization is pending.

## Recommended decision framing and bounded qualification gate

The human can settle the trade-off now, while implementation feasibility remains an honest gate:

**Recommended least additional assumption:** require contract-enforced wallet rights, exact canonical-byte/projection binding, public v1 state relations and atomic CAS. Require qualification of full required public-signature verification before production acceptance if avoiding the additional invalid-signature denial surface is a product requirement. Freeze qualified v1 bytes. Do not automatically switch to client-only signature verification when qualification fails.

If the human instead explicitly accepts the cheaper split-verification model's added denial/irrecoverability risk, freeze that as the contract with the precise scope and freeze/new-team recovery path above. This is a real security/availability choice, not a fact an agent should invent. Neither model promises correct encryption by malicious authorized writers.

One bounded **chain-verifier qualification** continuation can be stated now:

1. Pin the proposed registry/adapter schema and selected actual chain implementation/fork; identify the exact canonical bytes and public validation predicates. Differentially compare contract projections/actions against the immutable qualified corpus, plus adversarial projection, signature, actor, acceptance, membership, stale-head, sealed-head and overflow cases. No expected-vector regeneration to make code pass.
2. For full public verification, qualify strict Ed25519 equivalence and canonical point/R/subgroup rejection on the actual complete v1 message; reject any unsupported primitive or semantics mismatch. For split verification, demonstrate the authorized invalid-head failure and fail-closed recovery limitation explicitly.
3. Establish and freeze supported maximum team/vault/record/ref sizes from measured worst-case calldata, gas, computation/storage and transaction atomicity. No claim that all pure-core parser maxima fit one EVM transaction. Reducing product capacity must be explicit, preserve complete required ref sets and never truncate proofs.
4. Execute concurrent competing authority/content/removal/rotation transactions, idempotency/uncertain-send disclosure, fork/reorg/outage and independently fetched data checks. Bind client acceptance to the chosen finalized checkpoint rather than a database or unverified RPC field.
5. Demonstrate incident behavior without resetting accepted pins: recoverable historical data, explicit new identity/team transition where selected, stale/current distinction and unrecoverable-case denial. No hidden operator bypass.
6. Require independent review and exact source/artifact/target evidence. Missing target access or failed feasibility blocks dependent chain/client production implementation. Return to an explicit decision if the selected design cannot satisfy the gate; no silent crypto weakening, trusted signer, optimistic mechanism or private-key disclosure fallback.

This is sufficiently sharp to ticket. Qualification failure can reopen an explicitly identified trade-off; it need not remain vague map fog. It does not authorize transactions, resource provisioning/deployment, publication or changes to the legacy registry/core50. No deployable setting is introduced by this research note.
